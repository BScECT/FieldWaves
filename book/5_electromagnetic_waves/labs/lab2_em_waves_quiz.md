### Formative assessment

Airborne radars measure the thickness of ice sheets by sending 60 MHz waves down through the ice and timing the echo from the bedrock. Glacier ice is (approximately) lossless and non-magnetic. It responds to an electric field with a polarization density $\mathbf{P}=\epsilon_0\chi_e\mathbf{E}$, where $\chi_e=2.15$ is its electric susceptibility.

Inside the ice, the radar wave travels down, along $+z$, with magnetic field phasor

$$
\mathbf{H}(z)=H_0\,(\hat{x}-i\,\hat{y})\,e^{-ikz}\ \mathrm{A/m}.
$$

(a) Write the physical field $\mathbf{H}(z,t)$. How does the tip of $\mathbf{H}$ move in time at a fixed $z$? What is the polarization of this wave? (2 points)

(b) In the ice, the Ampère–Maxwell law becomes $\nabla\times\mathbf{H}=\frac{\partial\mathbf{D}}{\partial t}$, with $\mathbf{D}=\epsilon_0\mathbf{E}+\mathbf{P}$. Find $\mathbf{E}$ in phasor form, in terms of $H_0$, $\eta_0$ and $\chi_e$, where $\epsilon_r=1+\chi_e$. (2 points)

(c) What are $c_p$ and $\eta$ in terms of $c$, $\eta_0$ and $\chi_e$? What is the amplitude of $\mathbf{E}$ if $H_0=0.1$ A/m? (2 points)

(d) Find the phasor $\mathbf{P}(z)$. What fraction of $\mathbf{D}$ comes from the polarization density of the ice? (2 points)

(e) The bedrock echo arrives $20\ \mu\mathrm{s}$ after the echo from the ice surface. How thick is the ice? What thickness would you get if you ignored $\chi_e$? (2 points)
