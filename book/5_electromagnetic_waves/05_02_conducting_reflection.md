# Conducting reflection: the current generates the return field

The matching conditions in {doc}`05_01_normal_incidence` tell us how much reflects. Here we give the reflection a source: an electric field drives a current, and that current generates an additional field.

We met the first part in {doc}`04_03_conductors_and_skin_depth`, through $\vec J_c=\sigma\vec E$. We met currents generating magnetic fields much earlier in {doc}`../2_potential_fields/magnetic_field/from_currents_to_dipoles`. Now the current varies harmonically and generates outgoing electromagnetic waves.

## Start with a current whose value is given

Put an infinite current sheet at $z=0$, in an otherwise uniform, lossless medium with impedance $\eta$ and wavenumber $k$. Prescribe the surface-current phasor

$$
\vec K=\hat x K_0,\qquad [K_0]=\mathrm{A/m}.
$$

An external source maintains this current. There are initially no incident waves; we seek the fields emitted by the sheet. Translation symmetry along $x$ and $y$ suggests plane waves moving away in both $z$ directions. Write their electric fields as

$$
\vec E_1=\hat x A_-e^{+jkz}\quad(z<0),\qquad
\vec E_2=\hat x A_+e^{-jkz}\quad(z>0).
$$

Each satisfies the wave equation away from the sheet. The signs select **outgoing** waves. Tangential electric-field continuity gives $A_-=A_+=A_s$.

From the curl calculation on the previous page,

$$
\vec H_1=-\hat y\frac{A_s}{\eta}e^{+jkz},\qquad
\vec H_2=+\hat y\frac{A_s}{\eta}e^{-jkz}.
$$

The electric field has the same direction on the two sides at the sheet; the magnetic field changes direction because the waves propagate oppositely. Apply the current jump condition {eq}`eq:em-interface-h-jump`:

$$
\hat z\times\left(\hat y\frac{2A_s}{\eta}\right)
=-\hat x\frac{2A_s}{\eta}=\hat xK_0.
$$

Therefore

$$
\boxed{A_s=-\frac{\eta K_0}{2},\qquad
\vec E_{\mathrm{emitted}}(z)
=-\hat x\frac{\eta K_0}{2}e^{-jk|z|}.}
$$ (eq:em-interface-sheet-radiation)

We have found a field generated by a current. The homogeneous differential equation describes propagation on either side; the source enters through the jump between the two solutions. This is a boundary-value problem with a source condition.

## Now let the incident field drive the current

Replace the external current source by a thin conducting sheet and illuminate it from $z<0$:

$$
\vec E_i(z)=\hat x E_i e^{-jkz}.
$$

Use a simple sheet version of Ohm's law,

$$
\boxed{K_0=G_s E_{\mathrm{sheet}},}
$$

where $G_s\geq0$ is the **surface conductance**, in siemens. The units work: S times V/m gives A/m. For a thin film of thickness $h$ with approximately uniform field through its thickness, $G_s\approx\sigma h$. That approximation requires a film thin compared with its wavelength and skin depth; here the sheet law itself defines our idealized model.

What is $E_{\mathrm{sheet}}$? It is the **total tangential field at the sheet**, including the field generated by the current:

$$
E_{\mathrm{sheet}}=E_i+E_{\mathrm{emitted}}
=E_i-\frac{\eta K_0}{2}.
$$ (eq:em-interface-sheet-self-consistency)


The current responds to that sum. It would be inconsistent to use only $E_i$ in Ohm's law while ignoring the field the current generates.

Substitute $K_0=G_sE_{\mathrm{sheet}}$:

$$
\left(1+\frac{\eta G_s}{2}\right)E_{\mathrm{sheet}}=E_i,
\qquad
\boxed{E_{\mathrm{sheet}}=\frac{2E_i}{2+\eta G_s},\qquad
K_0=\frac{2G_sE_i}{2+\eta G_s}.}
$$

The backward emitted wave is the reflected wave. On the forward side, the emitted wave adds to the incident wave to give transmission. Thus

$$
\boxed{r=-\frac{\eta G_s}{2+\eta G_s},\qquad
 t=\frac{2}{2+\eta G_s}=1+r.}
$$ (eq:em-interface-sheet-coefficients)

This is the physical meaning of superposition here. The sheet emits to both sides. The forward contribution reduces the total transmitted electric field; the backward contribution provides the return signal.

You can explore this superposition and its limiting behaviour in {ref}`the activity below <em-conductor-activity>`.

## A perfect reflector can carry a finite current

Take the ideal limit $G_s\to\infty$:

$$
\boxed{r\to-1,\qquad t\to0,\qquad
E_{\mathrm{sheet}}\to0,\qquad K_0\to\frac{2E_i}{\eta}.}
$$ (eq:em-interface-perfect-conductor)

The total tangential electric field tends to zero while a finite surface current remains. We reach this result by taking a limit of the conducting response. Setting the field to zero first and then trying to infer the current from $G_sE$ would hide that limit.

At the incident surface the electric fields cancel, while the magnetic fields add:

$$
H_{1y}(0)=\frac{E_i}{\eta}-\frac{rE_i}{\eta}
\longrightarrow\frac{2E_i}{\eta},\qquad H_{2y}(0)\longrightarrow0.
$$

The surface-current condition gives $K_x=H_{1y}(0)$. This recovers the relation between tangential magnetic field and conductor surface current encountered in {doc}`02_03_transmission_lines`.

For a real good conductor, {doc}`04_03_conductors_and_skin_depth` gives a thin region of penetrating field and volume current. The surface current is an effective description of that concentrated current, $\vec K=\int\vec J_{\mathrm{tangent}}\,dz$. The perfect-conductor model captures the exterior reflection when penetration is negligible on the scales of interest.

## Where does the power go?

The medium on both sides of the sheet is the same, so

$$
\mathcal R=|r|^2,\qquad \mathcal T=|t|^2.
$$

For finite, real $G_s$, some incoming power heats the sheet. The mean heating per unit area is

$$
\langle p_{\mathrm{heat}}\rangle
=\frac12\operatorname{Re}\{E_{\mathrm{sheet}}K_0^*\}
=\frac{G_s}{2}|E_{\mathrm{sheet}}|^2.
$$

This is the surface version of the Joule-heating term in {doc}`04_03_conductors_and_skin_depth`. Divide by incident intensity $|E_i|^2/(2\eta)$ to obtain the absorbed fraction:

$$
\boxed{\mathcal A=\frac{4\eta G_s}{(2+\eta G_s)^2},\qquad
\mathcal R+\mathcal T+\mathcal A=1.}
$$ (eq:em-interface-sheet-energy)

As $G_s\to0$, the sheet disappears electromagnetically: $t\to1$. As $G_s\to\infty$, the ideal sheet reflects all the power and absorbs none. A larger conductance does not automatically mean more absorbed power; it also changes the field that drives the current.

## Make the conductor better

Keep the incident wave fixed and improve the conducting response. The activity has two models. **Thin sheet** follows the calculation above: both sides are open, so power can be reflected, transmitted or absorbed. **Thick conductor** fills the whole region $z>0$, with current spread through a skin layer and no back face.

For the thick conductor, we reuse the complex wave impedance from {doc}`04_03_conductors_and_skin_depth`. Viewed at the surface it is called the **surface impedance** $Z_s$:

$$
E_{\mathrm{surface}}=Z_sH_{\mathrm{surface}},\qquad
Z_s=\frac{\omega\mu}{k}
\approx\frac{1+j}{\sigma\delta_E}
\quad\text{for a good conductor}.
$$

The surface response has a phase as well as a resistance. The familiar matching result still applies,

$$
r=\frac{Z_s-\eta_0}{Z_s+\eta_0},\qquad
E_{\mathrm{surface}}=(1+r)E_i.
$$

We use the full complex $k$ in the activity, so its weaker-conductivity settings are also described consistently. The current shown for the thick conductor is $K=\int_0^\infty J_x(z)\,dz$, in A/m. At finite conductivity this is an integrated volume current; in the perfect-conductor limit it becomes the surface current discussed above. It approaches $2E_i/\eta_0$ while the surface electric field approaches zero.

The bulk power calculation links directly back to {doc}`03_02_energy_flow` and the Joule-heating term in {doc}`04_03_conductors_and_skin_depth`:

$$
\langle S_z(0)\rangle=\frac12\operatorname{Re}\{Z_s\}|H_{\mathrm{surface}}|^2
=\int_0^\infty\frac{\sigma}{2}|E_x(z)|^2\,dz.
$$

Power entering the conducting half-space is eventually deposited as heat. It is not power transmitted through a thin sheet into another lossless region. The activity keeps these two geometries separate; inserting a thick conductor's full thickness into $G_s=\sigma h$ would miss the skin-depth response.
