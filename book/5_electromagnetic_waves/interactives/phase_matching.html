Read the vector wavenumbers and the PDE check

Coordinates are displayed in units of the wavelength λ₁ in medium 1. The drawing has equal spatial scales along x and z. Wavevector components below are normalized by k₁; arrows in the drawing indicate direction. Angles are measured from the interface normal, the z axis.

Equations and assumptions

Medium 1 is air, with εᵣ₁ = 1; both media are isotropic, nonmagnetic and lossless. The fixed frequency is 100 MHz. Thus k₂/k₁ = √εᵣ₂ and v₂/v₁ = 1/√εᵣ₂. We keep εᵣ₂ ≥ 1 so a propagating transmitted wave exists throughout the incidence-angle range.

Incident kᵢ/k₁ = sin θᵢ x̂ + cos θᵢ ẑ; reflected kᵣ/k₁ = sin θᵢ x̂ − cos θᵢ ẑ. Transmitted kₜ/k₁ = √εᵣ₂(sin θₜ x̂ + cos θₜ ẑ). Each proposed wave has the allowed magnitude in its own medium, even when the chosen transmitted angle fails the boundary condition.

At z = 0 the common time factor leaves spatial phases −kᵢ,ₓ x, −kᵣ,ₓ x and −kₜ,ₓ x. Matching the entire boundary requires kᵢ,ₓ = kᵣ,ₓ = kₜ,ₓ. The automatic setting solves sin θₜ = sin θᵢ/√εᵣ₂. The reflected angle equals the incident angle. Crest spacing along the boundary is 2π/|kₓ|; at kₓ = 0 there is no phase variation along it.

All phase factors are one at the origin before restoring the common time factor. Changing a constant phase offset could move that agreement point, but it cannot repair unequal phase gradients along the interface. A near-match label means less than 0.05 rad phase mismatch over the displayed tangential interval; exact matching along the whole interface still requires equal tangential wavenumbers.
