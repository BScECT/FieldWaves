Field, voltage and power

With the common factor e−jkz understood, the incident phasors are E/E₀ = x̂ (linear) and E/E₀ = (x̂ + jŷ)/√2 (circular). Both have the same E · E* = E₀² and incident average flux E₀²/(2η₀). Circular handedness is left unlabelled until a viewing convention is specified.

Antenna axis: â = sin β cos θ x̂ + sin β sin θ ŷ + cos β ẑ.
Normalized voltage phasor: q = (E/E₀) · â.
Real instantaneous voltage: v/vref,peak = Re{q ejωt}.
Average received power: P/Pref = |q|², where Pref is the average power for the aligned linear signal with the same receiver and incident intensity.

The voltage plot and power bar keep fixed scales. Rotating an ideal linear antenna within the transverse plane receives half the reference power from the circular signal; tilting it along propagation gives zero. This is a comparison with an ideal linear antenna, while GNSS receiver antennas are commonly designed for circular polarization.
