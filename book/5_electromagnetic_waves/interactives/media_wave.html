A wave can fade without spreading

Hold the electric field just inside the material fixed. Start with zero conductivity: dielectric loss alone can attenuate the wave. The common harmonic time factor is eʲωᵗ.

Blue: real E / E₀ · Orange: real H / |H(0)| · Grey dashed: ±e⁻ᵅᶻ envelopes. Time is slowed for viewing.

Changing the material can change S(0), because E₀ is held fixed. The power graph is S(z)/S(0), so it isolates decay inside each material. Field and power axes stay fixed. Frequency, length and conductivity sliders are logarithmic.

Equations and assumptions

εd/ε₀ = er − j er × loss ratio. εeff/ε₀ = er − j[er × loss ratio + σ/(ωε₀)]. k² = ω²μ₀εeff, k = β − jα with α ≥ 0, β > 0. E(z)/E₀ = e⁻ᵅᶻe⁻ʲᵝᶻ and H(z) = k E(z)/(ωμ₀). The curves show their real parts after multiplication by eʲωᵗ. H has its own peak normalization so its phase is visible; its plotted height is not its SI magnitude relative to E.

S(z)/S(0) = e⁻²ᵅᶻ. The dielectric and conduction heating fractions are ωε″d/(ωε″d+σ) and σ/(ωε″d+σ). The medium is homogeneous, isotropic and has real μ=μ₀. These independent controls specify a single-frequency teaching model, not a fitted material spectrum. No reflection, geometric spreading or scattering is included.
