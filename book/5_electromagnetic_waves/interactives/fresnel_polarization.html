Equations, signed amplitudes and assumptions

Medium 1 is air (n₁ = 1). Both media are lossless, isotropic and nonmagnetic, so n₂ = √εᵣ₂, η₂/η₁ = 1/n₂ and sin θₜ = sin θᵢ/n₂. The electric amplitudes follow the H/V basis conventions in the text. ℛ = r²; 𝒯 = n₂ cos θₜ/cos θᵢ × t². Transmission is calculated independently, rather than assigned as 1−ℛ.

For n₂ ≠ n₁, θB = arctan(n₂/n₁) and θB + θₜ = 90°. At εᵣ₂ = 1 there is no interface contrast: neither polarization reflects at any angle, so no unique Brewster angle is shown. The control stops at 89° to keep a nonzero incident normal power flux; the curves approach grazing incidence.

For unit total incident unpolarized power, the reflected H and V powers are ℛH/2 and ℛV/2. An ideal lens with axis α from V transmits [ℛH sin²α + ℛV cos²α]/2. The two independent polarization powers add; their field amplitudes are not added coherently.

The glass and water presets use approximate visible-light indices; liquid water's radar-frequency permittivity is very different. The radar preset is a separate illustrative lossless dielectric. These are single planar-interface calculations at a chosen frequency, not a rough-surface backscatter or real-lens model. Roughness, absorption and imperfect polarizers can prevent complete glare extinction.
