# Introduction to Potential Fields

How can a scalar function describe a vector field? We begin with gravity and the physical ideas of work and conservation of energy, then build the connection between a potential and its gradient.

The opening historical examples introduce a problem that needs explaining: the gravitational field of a spherical shell. Work in a simpler near-surface field gives us a route toward conservative fields and potential, before returning to more complicated sources.

The chapter then applies this language to gravity, electrostatics and Earth's magnetic field. The sources and physical responses differ, but gradients, superposition, Laplace's equation and boundary conditions provide reusable tools. In each setting, ask what the potential represents and where the description is valid.
