# Making the Earth-Field Figures

The Earth-dipole comparisons and spherical-pattern figures are generated by [plot_earth_dipole.py](plot_earth_dipole.py). The calculation uses NumPy and Matplotlib; it does not require a geomagnetic package or a live data service.

## Data and conventions

The stored [IGRF-14 coefficient table](data/igrf14coeffs.txt) is the official IAGA table distributed by NOAA/NCEI, downloaded on 17 September 2026 {cite}`igrf14_coefficients`. The figures use **epoch 2025.0**, without extrapolation. The table's original units are nanotesla, and the reference radius is $a=6371.2\,\mathrm{km}$.

The spherical-harmonic synthesis uses Schmidt semi-normalized associated Legendre functions without a Condon–Shortley phase. It evaluates all degrees through 13 for the reference main field and only degree 1 for the equivalent centred tilted dipole. Geographic plots use **geocentric latitude**, longitude east, and the spherical surface $r=a$. They do not claim ellipsoidal sea-level or local topographic coordinates. Reported radial components are positive outward.

The plotted surface residual is $B_{r,\mathrm{IGRF}}-B_{r,\mathrm{dip}}$. The three maps share a colour scale. IGRF is an observation-based **main-field model**, not a complete measurement of core, crustal and external fields at one instant.

## Reproduce the figures

From the repository root, in the `fieldwaves` environment:

```bash
python book/2_potential_fields/magnetic_field/plot_earth_dipole.py
```

The script writes SVG and PNG figures and [a numerical check report](data/dipole_checks.json). The coordinate schematic exaggerates the dipole tilt for readability; the comparison calculation uses the actual degree-one coefficients. The angular-pattern panels are mathematical examples with arbitrary amplitudes, not observational maps.

## Checks before plotting

The script checks the following:

- The degree-one synthesis agrees with the independent Cartesian point-dipole expression at several locations.
- All three full-model field components agree with finite differences of the scalar potential, including the spherical-coordinate scale factors.
- Integration over a sphere gives zero net radial magnetic flux.
- Surface integration of the vector field's square agrees with the coefficient-based mean-square spectrum.
- The full mean-square field equals the dipole contribution plus the residual contribution.

For Schmidt semi-normalized coefficients at $r=a$, the degree-$\ell$ mean-square contribution is

$$
R_\ell=(\ell+1)\sum_{m=0}^{\ell}
\left[(g_\ell^m)^2+(h_\ell^m)^2\right].
$$

The script independently evaluates the surface integrals using Gauss–Legendre quadrature in cosine of colatitude and uniform longitude sampling. For epoch 2025.0 it obtains

$$
\frac{R_1}{\sum_{\ell=1}^{13}R_\ell}=0.928616\ldots.
$$

This defines the approximately 93% statement in the chapter. The non-dipole vector RMS is about 27% of the full vector RMS, even though its share of the mean square is only about 7%; these are different statistics.

The code and data are included so that the numerical claims can be reproduced. Understanding the synthesis algorithm is not a prerequisite for the chapter's dipole, boundary-condition or attenuation exercises.

## Reproduce the charge-pair explorer

The {doc}`dipole_explorer` notebook contains the complete Python calculation and an embedded browser display. The controls work in the built book without a kernel or external plotting service. Download and run all cells in Jupyter to reproduce both the interactive display and the decay plots. NumPy, Matplotlib and IPython are sufficient.

The six panels use the exact point-charge formulas, adding potentials as scalars and fields as vectors. A separate ideal-dipole formula is compared at a movable probe. The notebook checks symmetry, agreement between the field and numerical potential gradients, convergence to the far-field approximation, and the asymptotic decay slopes. Sources are masked near their singularities; they are not numerically softened.

All potential panels use one colour scale; all field panels use one arrow scale. The captions explain the colour stretch and arrow clipping. The fixed-charge and fixed-moment controls describe different experiments: at fixed charge, reducing separation reduces the moment; at fixed moment, the charge must increase.
