# Exercises on travel times of seismic wave fields

1. In {numref}`fig-raypath-ex1` below, a ray is reflected multiple times (twice) between the top and bottom boundary. A source is located at the origin $(0,0)$ and a receiver at position $(x,0)$. For this ray:

   - **Derive** the travel time as a function of horizontal distance $x$;
   - **Plot** this travel time as a function of the distance $x$.

   ```{figure} figures/raypaths_multiples_for_exercise.png
   :width: 85%

   Ray path for a multiply reflected wave.
   ```

2. The earth is 3-D and common recordings are done over an area, retrieving 3-D information from the subsurface, the recording time being the third dimension. A typical situation is sketched in {numref}`fig-raypath-ex2` below where a source is away from a receiver line. For a direct wave from the source to receiver:

   - **Derive** the travel time as a function of the horizontal distances $x$ and $y$;
   - **Plot** the travel time as a function of the horizontal distance $y$ only.

   ```{figure} figures/raypaths_3D_for_exercise.png
   :name: fig-raypath-ex2
   :width: 85%

   Ray paths of direct waves for 3D recordings; top view.
   ```
